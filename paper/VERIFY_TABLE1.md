# Table 1 needs verification before submission

The related-work comparison table is built from each study's published account
of its own design, drawn from abstracts and methods sections. Some cells are
firmer than others, and the difference matters because a wrong cell in a
comparison table is the kind of error a cited author notices immediately.

## Cells we are confident about

Unit of analysis, observational versus survey, and longitudinal versus
cross-sectional. These are stated plainly in every one of the ten studies.

Specifically verified: Xia et al. (2021) analyse three real datasets at both
overall and aspect level and model gradual and sudden change, so longitudinal
is correct and event-based is not. Mittal et al. (1999) use a longitudinal
survey of 5,206 automobile owners, so observational is "no". de Matos et al.
(2007) is a meta-analysis of service recovery studies, predominantly scenario
based, so "mix" under longitudinal and "no" under observational.

## Cells that need checking

**Null** and **Density** are recorded as "no" throughout the prior work. This
reflects that we found no such reporting in the published accounts, not a
verified absence. Read the methods section of each before submission. If any
study does report a resampling null or a feasibility diagnostic, the cell must
change and the surrounding text with it, because the paragraph after the table
asserts that no study in either group reports density.

**Event** for de Matos et al. (2007) is marked "yes" because the service
recovery literature is organised around failure and recovery episodes. Confirm
this framing holds for the studies in that meta-analysis rather than only for
the construct.

## What we deliberately did not do

No cell was filled by inference from a paper's title or venue. Where we could
not establish a property, the honest options were to check it or to drop the
column, and we kept only columns we could fill from the retrieved text.

The "attempted" entry in the final row is not a hedge. We specified a
time-to-event analysis and did not run it; marking it "yes" would claim a
contribution we did not make.
